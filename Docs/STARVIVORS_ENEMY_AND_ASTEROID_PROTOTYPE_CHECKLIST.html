Goal And Success Criteria

Each live enemy should read as a distinct combat role before it damages the player. The player should be able to understand what the enemy is about to do, where the danger will occur, when the action is unsafe, and when the enemy has recovered or died. Asteroids should remain simple world hazards with varied asteroid-like shapes and clear collision feedback.

Every live enemy has a role-specific silhouette at gameplay scale.
Every dangerous enemy action has a visible telegraph before the damage window.
Attack VFX show origin, path or area, impact, and recovery state.
Audio cues reinforce high-risk telegraphs without masking player feedback.
Hit, death, split, summon, shield, repair, and status feedback are distinct.
Live gameplay validation passes in dense fights, not only in isolated previews.

Prototype Visual Rules

Rule Acceptance Notes Done
Readable state changes Windup, active, recovery, shielded, damaged, enraged, summoning, repairing, and fleeing states are visible without reading debug text.
Clear telegraphs Lines, arcs, rings, pulses, cones, and countdowns use consistent timing and enough contrast against the world.
Attack trails Projectiles, beams, dashes, shockwaves, mines, and area attacks leave short-lived path information the player can parse.
Recovery feedback After a high-risk attack, the enemy shows a readable cooldown or vulnerability beat before returning to pressure.
Role-specific silhouettes Fast, heavy, ranged, support, spawn, status, stealth, and loot enemies are identifiable by shape at HUD/gameplay scale.
Live-game validation Readability is checked in real runs with HUD, pickups, shots, asteroids, squads, and accessibility settings active.
